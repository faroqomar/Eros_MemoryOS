# MemoryOS: three interactive design studies

Open **index.html** in a browser. It contains all three demos and needs no server, installation, or internet.
Choose Still, Daybook, or Commonplace. Each keeps its own sample changes until you restart or reload.
The three numbered HTML files also open independently.

- **Still:** capture first, a clear voice action, quiet recent memories.
- **Daybook:** a warm writing surface and dated journal entries.
- **Commonplace:** familiar people, projects, and ideas, with capture close by.

Try writing a thought, finding a person, checking an original source, and pausing/saving a sample recording.
The menu also opens projects, birthdays, connections, import previews, and privacy controls.
Recording, audio, import, reminders, and answers are labeled simulations. No microphone, files, accounts,
cloud service, or real AI are connected. Fictional examples stay separate from the native app.
Arabic controls and RTL are previews; some example content keeps its original language.

Independent AI heuristic review rated each design at least 9/10 in Typography, Hierarchy, Spacing & density,
Color & contrast, Iconography, Consistency & polish, and Clarity after repairs. These are expert judgments,
not measured usability-study results or owner acceptance. Real-user and physical-iPhone checks remain open.
See [independent review](evidence/review.md) and [source identities](evidence/source-identity.json).

Reference preparation used the supplied eight-screen archive, Jon Yablonski’s official Laws of UX website,
the publisher’s second-edition preview, and direct review of the supplied 220-page PDF after download.
The book prompted final visible labels for key icons. The project task record preserves the page map,
review scores, repairs, checks, and limits.
